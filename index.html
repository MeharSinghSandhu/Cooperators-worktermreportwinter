Hi, I am Mehar !

This past winter, I spent my co-op term at Co-operators in Guelph, working as an Associate Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) Specialist.

If you aren't a tech geek, you might be wondering: What does an SRE actually do? Think of me as a digital "first responder" and architect. My job was to make sure our systems stayed healthy and fast.

Through this experience, I worked extensively with Dynatrace to design dashboards, configure alerting, and implement privacy-focused solutions like PII masking in session replays. Who are Cooperators ? 🤔

Co-operators is a Canadian financial services organization that provides insurance and investment products. The company operates large-scale digital systems that support customer-facing platforms such as claims processing, online quoting, policy management, and payment services.

The Digital Technology Operations (DTO) team focuses on maintaining the reliability, performance, and security of these systems. My work was closely aligned with .NET apis and looking at overall systems in place, making observability and system monitoring a critical part of the organization's operations.

What I Did

During my work term, I worked as part of the Digital Technology Operations (DTO) team, focusing on improving system observability, reliability, and data security across enterprise applications.

01

User Action Naming

  • Designed and refined user action naming rules in Dynatrace to improve visibility into frontend user interactions.
  • Standardized naming conventions for better tracking and analysis across applications.
  • Helped teams better understand user behavior and performance bottlenecks.
02

PII Masking (Session Replay)

  • Implemented targeted masking rules to prevent exposure of sensitive customer data (e.g., payment and banking details) in session replays.
  • Used semantic selectors and DOM-based targeting to ensure only sensitive fields were masked without impacting usability.
  • Validated masking across multiple user flows in pre-production before production rollout.
03

Dashboard Creation

  • Built and maintained Dynatrace dashboards to monitor application health, performance, and reliability.
  • Created visualizations for key metrics such as request volume, failure rates, and response times.
  • Improved dashboard usability by adding environment filters and organizing data for different teams.
04

Alerting & Key Request Tuning

  • Configured alerting profiles and thresholds to detect service issues early.
  • Tuned key requests to focus monitoring on critical application endpoints.
  • Reduced noise by refining alert conditions and ensuring alerts were actionable.

Some Interesting Stories Here

⏱️

Story 1: The Case of the Vanishing 3D Models

We were literally packing our bags at the Mississauga office, ready to head back to Guelph, when the "emergency" call came in — the 3D car models on the claims website had turned into nothing but black shadows. They weren't rendering at all.

SREs to the rescue. Instead of guessing, my team and I went into full detective mode. We started slicing the data:

Was it everyone?

Just Android? iOS? Desktop?

When exactly did the first "shadow" appear?

By analyzing the session data, we found the culprit: a browser update from a week prior had changed the rules for how 3D models were rendered. We handed our findings over to the dev team on a silver platter. What could have taken days of "trial and error" to find was diagnosed and resolved in mere hours because we had the data to back it up.

💬

Story 2: The Bilingual Chatbot Blunder

The team was stuck on a frustrating bug — users on the French version of the site were still getting the English chatbot. To make matters worse, the Dynatrace session replays were a mess of long, unreadable URLs — it was impossible to tell what the users were actually doing.

I jumped in and implemented User Actions in Dynatrace. Within minutes, those messy URLs turned into clear, readable steps (like "Clicked: Contact Support"). This allowed the team to filter for exact sessions and see the problem instantly — even though the code pointed to a French URL, the actual background request (XHR) was still stubbornly reaching out to the English one. By cleaning up the data, I saved the team dozens of hours and kept the project under budget.

📈

Story 3: Stress-Free Releases

Usually, "Release Day" is the most stressful time for an engineering team. Everyone is holding their breath, asking — "Did we break it? Is it working?"

I wanted to change that "wait and see" culture, so I built a Release Command Center Dashboard. I mapped out all the APIs affected by the release and created simple "Success vs. Failure" graphs.

Instead of panic, we had clarity. Within seconds of a release going live, we could look at one screen and see if the requests were green or red. The team praised the dashboard because it turned a high-stress guessing game into a calm, data-driven process.
